# PI Shortlist

Pulled from author/affiliation data on real papers found during the PFAS enzyme-engineering literature search (via PubMed E-utilities, not a general search engine). Ranked by fit to a concrete ask: cell-free expression plus a fluoride-release assay on 5 to 10 AI-designed sequences.

**Caveat that applies to all four:** these are affiliations and emails as published in the specific papers cited. They may be outdated. There's no way to know current lab capacity, funding, or whether any of them would be open to an unsolicited pitch from outside academia. This is a real, literature-grounded starting list, not a vetted list of people who've agreed to anything.

**Status:** #1 and #2 were emailed on 2026-08-15 (see `email_template.md`). No response yet as of this writing.

## 1. Peter R. Jaffé — Princeton University

Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering. `jaffe@princeton.edu`

**Why he's first:** his group did the actual gene-knockout work proving `rdhA` is required for real PFOA/PFOS defluorination in *Acidimicrobium* sp. strain A6 (Jaffé et al., 2024). He's the person most directly working the "does a real enzyme actually do this" question, the exact question this project's designs are trying to answer computationally.

Worth noting: his lab does field environmental engineering, not necessarily hands-on protein expression. He may be the right person to ask whether `rdhA` is close to going public, and not the right person for the expression/assay work itself.

## 2. Lawrence P. Wackett — University of Minnesota

Department of Biochemistry, Molecular Biology and Biophysics. `wacke003@umn.edu`

**Why he's second:** his paper, "Microwell fluoride assay screening for enzymatic defluorination," is a high-throughput fluoride-detection assay for exactly this kind of screening, the specific method this project's ask needs. If anyone already has the assay running, it's this lab. Arguably the stronger technical fit for the actual ask, even though his own PFAS-specific publication record is thinner than Jaffé's.

## 3. Carla Ng — University of Pittsburgh

Department of Chemical and Petroleum Engineering / Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering.

**Why she's third:** co-author on "Mechanisms and Opportunities for Rational In Silico Design of Enzymes to Degrade PFAS." Her lab is already thinking about computational PFAS enzyme design specifically, which makes an AI-generated candidate set an on-thesis pitch rather than a cold one.

## 4. Colin Scott — CSIRO Environment, Canberra, Australia

Co-author on "Toward the development of a molecular toolkit for the microbial remediation of PFAS."

**Why he's fourth:** real, relevant work, but CSIRO is a government research organization rather than a university lab, likely a different (probably slower) engagement path than the other three, and international.
